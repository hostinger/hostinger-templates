**Mara:** This is Room Tone. I'm Mara Voss. Episode nine: Kingdom of the Model Railway. In a barn outside town, Wilf Adeyemi has spent twenty-two years building a railway empire at one seventy-sixth scale, timetabled to the minute, with a working post office and a strike that lasted, in scale time, eleven days.

**Wilf:** It is not a train set. A train set goes in circles. This is a working railway that happens to be small. Every wagon has a destination and a reason. If the six-forty runs late, somewhere on the layout a tiny factory misses its coal.

**Mara:** You gave your own railway a labor dispute?

**Wilf:** The signalmen wanted a tea hut. I had painted the yard before I could afford to detail the interiors, you see.

**Wilf:** So I ran a slow service for eleven scale days, and then I built them their hut. It is the best building on the layout.

**Mara:** Twenty-two years. Was there a plan at the start?

**Wilf:** There was a junction. That is all a railway needs to become an argument with yourself. One junction implies two destinations, two destinations imply industry, industry implies people. My wife says I didn't build a railway, I incorporated a small nation.

**Mara:** The timetable runs to real minutes. What happens when you get it wrong?

**Wilf:** The same thing that happens on the real thing: everything downstream inherits your mistake. I once mislaid a cattle wagon for three months. When it turned up behind the paint shop I gave it a welcome-home run, empty, flags on the buffer beam.

**Mara:** Does anyone else ever get to drive?

**Wilf:** Second Saturdays. Four operators, headsets, proper block working. Grown adults asking permission to enter a section of track in a barn in a field. It is the most orderly place in the county, and I include the church.
