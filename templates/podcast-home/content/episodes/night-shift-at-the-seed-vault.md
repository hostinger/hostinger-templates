**Mara:** This is Room Tone. I'm Mara Voss. Episode eleven: Night Shift at the Seed Vault. Six hundred miles above the Arctic Circle there is a concrete wedge set into a mountainside, and inside it, more than a million seed samples sleeping at minus eighteen degrees. Dr. Halvard Bruun has spent nine winters walking its corridors at two in the morning.

**Halvard:** People imagine alarms and lasers. Mostly it is listening. The vault has a sound when it is healthy, a low steady breath from the cooling plant. My job on the night shift is to notice when the breath changes.

**Mara:** Has it ever changed?

**Halvard:** Twice. Once it was a fan bearing, nothing at all. Once it was meltwater at the entrance tunnel, which made the newspapers sound like the end of the world.

**Halvard:** We laid new drainage, and the mountain went back to sleep. The seeds never knew.

**Mara:** What is actually on the shelves? Walk me down an aisle.

**Halvard:** Gray steel shelving, sealed foil packets in plastic crates, and the labels are the poetry. Barley from a rooftop in Aleppo. Beans a family carried through three border crossings. A rice variety that exists nowhere else, because the valley that grew it is under a reservoir now.

**Mara:** Do depositors ever come back for their seeds?

**Halvard:** Once, seriously, and it proved the whole idea. A gene bank lost its collection in a war and drew its own seeds back out of the mountain. Two years later they had regrown and redeposited most of it. The vault is not a tomb. It is a copy, the way a great library keeps a copy.

**Mara:** Nine winters of night shifts. What keeps you signing up for them?

**Halvard:** In February the sun does not rise at all, and the aurora stands over the mountain like a curtain in a draft. I let myself out at six in the morning, and for a moment I am the only person on earth who heard a million seeds get through the night. That is a good job.
